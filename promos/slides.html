The calendar

An entry is
not an event

A piece has up to four dates worth seeing: due, publishing, expiring, review. Each has its own lane, so one piece turns up on several days.

  • Drag a pip and the lane decides which date moves: the deadline in due, the post date in publishing
  • Unpublished drafts are on it, in italics
  • Colour is the stage. Red means late.

Editorial stages

A stage is
not a status

Craft answers “is this public?” Publishr answers “where is this in the process?” It keeps its answer next to Craft’s and never rewrites it.

  • Your own stages: rename, recolour, reorder
  • Turn Publishr off and every entry is exactly as public as it was
  • Every move written to an append-only history: who, when, from where

Publish requirements Pro

Checked at
sign-off, not
at save

Fields, word count, alt text, open comments, boxes a person ticks, and a clean pass from RedPen. Required ones block the move; advisory ones warn.

  • “I don’t know” is never “no.” A requirement that can’t run is skipped, and skipped never blocks.
  • Overrides need a permission, and the name goes in the history

Freshness and reporting Pro

Nothing tells
you a page has
gone stale

A policy says how long a kind of content stays good for. The clock runs from the last human review, or from the post date, so the first sweep finds the real backlog.

  • Typical age per stage is a median, not a mean
  • CSV export, one row per piece
  • One digest a morning, only to people with something in it
